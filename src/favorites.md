I was born in Poland in 2003, 14 years after the end of the Polish People's Republic, i.e. far away enough that I have no direct claim to the experience of communism, but close enough that much of the country I grew up in had been built by it. It'd be too simplistic to say that this background made me a minimalist ([Stalinist architecture of the early 1950s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_Culture_and_Science) isn't exactly an argument for visual modesty), but the questions it brought - the economy of means' "how, with limited space / resources, can we [make the product / space do more without becoming complicated / confusing?](https://culture.pl/pl/dzielo/mebloscianka-boguslawa-i-czeslaw-kowalscy)" -  still shaped much of my environment. Combined with the excess of the web / mass media from when I first started coding in the early 2010s, it resulted in a question of how abundance can be made "good" without becoming too much (can it at all?). 

The above is a software consideration today. A system/UI can technically contain everything a person needs and still feel hostile in the same way a badly organized building can be correct structurally and yet remain unpleasant to inhabit. There's a difference between a flat and a codebase, yes, but both can reveal whether the person making them cared about the experience of an average person using them. "Less is more"! In this case, at least. It's become too polished to mean very much, but the general idea, that complexity should have to justify itself, stands. 

Most of my aesthetic preferences follow from that. I like minimal interfaces, palettes, and typography, but don’t believe utility should come at the expense of beauty. Aesthetic value is often as important as practical one! That said, the two should be developed together. Beauty can be noticed only if the system underneath is clear and usable; once it starts adding noise or complexity for its own sake, it becomes merely an inconvenience. 

Besides SWE / product engineering, I'm also interested in architecture (per above), capital markets, and geopolitics, the latter against my own will. I write about these sometimes [here](/#/writing).
